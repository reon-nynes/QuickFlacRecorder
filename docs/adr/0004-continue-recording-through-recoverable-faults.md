# Continue recording through recoverable faults

Accepted during the design interview on 2026-10-15 and amended after the owner's clarification. Small errors such as losing samples allow the current recording to continue, with missing audio omitted and documented in an accompanying recording log. A reopened device may continue the same FLAC when its format and selected inputs remain compatible. When an input-device change makes the existing FLAC incompatible, such as a mid-recording input stream rate change, advance the counter automatically and start a new recording with the reopened device instead of forcing the new stream into the old FLAC.

An error-free recording produces only a FLAC, and the first error creates the recording log with timestamped diagnostics. Rollover logs in both recordings link their filenames. Inability to write a required recording log stops recording immediately. Capture recovery starts immediately, then at three and six seconds if needed, and stops after ten seconds without progress or three failed attempts. Repeated sample-loss notifications do not consume attempts while valid audio continues to save, and opening another filename does not reset the allowance; successfully saving incoming audio does.

Storage and encoder failures receive three retries following the triggering failure. The owner confirmed during implementation of issue #14 that the ten-second no-progress deadline also ends storage recovery, even when fewer than three retries have completed. Retries must happen before an output failure makes the FLAC encoder unusable, and partial native writes resume at their unwritten bytes. Stop cancels recovery and automatic rollover, and each new recording resets its displayed timer. This replaces the original stop-on-first-fault policy while preserving the distinction between missing samples, incompatible device changes, and errors that prevent required logging.

The owner confirmed the existing RecordingSession boundary with real temporary FLAC/log files, together with InputController and presentation/UI boundaries, for automated recovery validation. These checks do not replace native driver and hardware validation.
